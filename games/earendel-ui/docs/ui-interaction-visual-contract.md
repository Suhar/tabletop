# Earendel UI interaction visual contract

## Visual intents

- **Ship placement.** While the acting player holds a ship, the hex under the pointer shows a ghost of the ship that would land (a station docked to the stations it would link to), but only on a hex that accepts it. A fighter ghost also marks what it could attack from there with a pair of red chevrons on each shared edge, pushing toward the target; a probe ghost marks the planets it could survey the same way in pale blue. Every pair pulses in the same phase. An ordinary placement marks no hex at all: the player knows where a ship may go, and the ghost answers it. Only a special placement, an armed Gamma Torpedo or the ship owed on a new tile, marks its hexes with the turning ring; no hex is ever marked by an outline. On touch the first tap on a legal hex stages that hex as the preview (ghost, arcs and pointers shown there) and a second tap on the same hex places; any other legal hex moves the preview.
- **Choosing the ship.** With more than one ship in hand, a ship button in the action panel picks which one is placed; choosing it again puts it down. A special piece is chosen the same way and replaces the draw: once the discard is made, the player's special pieces join the hand row after an "or", as cards of the same size and style as the drawn ships (the unit's shape and name in the player's color, a white edge when chosen, hover text saying the draw goes back in the bag), fading in as the drawn cards slide over to make room; they are offered only until a drafted ship is placed. A ship owed after a laid tile cannot be swapped.
- **Tile laying.** Every legal origin for the tile at its current rotation shows a dashed outline of the four-hex footprint, its origin hex brighter. Hovering an origin (on touch, the first tap) fills the footprint with the tile's planet color at its planet hex; clicking (on touch, the second tap) lays it. Rotate buttons turn the tile.
- **Powers with two ends.** Tactical Shift, Warp Drive, Dimensional Rift, Spoofed Orders, Tactical Feint, Trade Route Wormhole and Immediate Download are chosen from the action panel. The board then marks only the near ends (own ships with an enemy beside them, own ships with a route, own Download planets with a technology to take within three hexes), then, once one is chosen, only the far ends. A marked hex carries a bright yellow ring around it with eight teeth pointing inward, the whole ring turning slowly; no hex is ever marked by a pulsing border. Rings are drawn in their own layer above every piece, beam and girder, below only the popups. The chosen near end keeps its ring, turned gray, so the yellow rings are only the far ends; no hex is filled in a power's color. Choosing the chosen end again puts it down; choosing another near end moves the choice. With a single near end it is chosen automatically.
- **Elevated technologies.** The setup draw's elevated cards sit side by side above the side panels as mini cards, each just the planet and the name in the planet's color, a discovered one dimmed. Clicking one deals the full cards out into a column down the left of the board, each sliding in from behind the left panel to its place, 450ms apiece and 100ms apart, the column clipped at the panel's edge so nothing shows over it (local motion, since nothing in the game changes); hovering a dealt card grows it about two and a half times from its left edge out over the board, and a click keeps it grown (or lets it go); clicking anywhere else, a mini card, or Escape fades them out in place. Each card is a landscape card in its planet's color with Event or Global in the band, the planet at the left, and the name and ability beside it, growing taller when the ability needs the room, as the special units' cards do; a discovered global reads Active at the left of its band (and carries an amber line under its mini card), a discovered event is dimmed and reads Resolved there. Discovering one puts a dot of every player's color in orbit around the planet. An event then owns the board: while the discoverer orders the others, the panel lists them as pills to click in sequence with a Confirm button, and Undo takes the last click back; while a player picks, the hexes they may take carry the turning ring (bombardment targets, the storm's pieces then its landing hexes, the planets to intercept), Salvage Rights lists the discard as choosable chips and Shipyard World offers one of each unit in play as buttons, each going once. Everyone else's prompt names who is picking. Free Drift's step uses the technology-move screen. Whenever a global in force is changing what the acting player sees (no discard under Overdrive, the free step under Free Drift, an escorted target in reach under No Quarter, a shot pending under Global Recycling, rivals' probes beside their claims under Shared Science), a muted line under the prompt says so, with the global's name as a chip whose popup explains it.
- **One-end powers.** Updated Logistics marks the fighters that have something in reach with the same turning ring; a click wakes one. Full Torpedo Spread lets the player toggle targets among the pending shot's targets, each picked one filled orange, and fires from the panel once two are picked. Arming the Railgun marks with the turning ring the targets it would change, which keep their red target chevrons. Arming the Gamma Torpedo, offered as a chip in the power column beside the hand whenever a held fighter has a station beside some legal hex, whether or not that fighter is the ship chosen (arming chooses it), narrows the placement rings to those hexes; the ghost becomes a single red-tipped dart and its chevrons point at the stations it could strike. Placing it commits the torpedo, which stands as that dart until it strikes: with one station beside it the strike is immediate, with several the stations carry the red target chevrons and Hold fire is not offered.
- **Free Drift's step.** The one move a turn can owe: while it is, the board marks the movable pieces with the turning ring, then, once one is picked up (its ring turns gray), its destinations, with a Hold position button beside them.
- **One-shot technologies.** Tactical Shift, Spoofed Orders and Beyond the Frontier are kept until spent and offered as power chips whenever a technology may be used. Tactical Shift is a power with two ends (the player's piece, then the empty hex beside it), and so is Spoofed Orders (a rival's piece beside one of the player's own, then the empty hex beside it to send it into); Beyond the Frontier commits on the chip's click, drawing the tile and the ship that will follow it; the prompt names that ship while the tile is being laid ("you drew a level 3 probe to place on the new tile"), and the tile is laid as below. Neither is ever owed, so neither has a button to decline it.
- **Pending decisions.** Survey candidates get pale blue chevrons from the probe; a pending shot gets red chevrons at each target, all pulsing in phase. A click on a candidate surveys it. The action panel's prompt carries its choices as small yellow pills with dark text inside the sentence, as Löwenherz does with its own colors: with a single candidate, survey and skip; with several, skip after asking for a click.
- **Special units in play.** Under the elevated cards, a row of five mini cards in the same style, one per category edged in the category's color, each the unit's board shape and its name, naming the special unit in play for that category (the hub, terraformer and relay are units like the rest); hovering one names the technology and its category. Clicking one deals the units' full cards out exactly as the elevated cards deal (the same column, slide, growth on hover and fade on dismiss): each a landscape card edged in the category's color with the granting technology in its band, the unit's shape at the left, and its name and what it does on the table beside it. The row is absent when the game option keeps every special piece in the deck. Each row carries a caption beneath it, "Enhanced techs in play" and "Special units in play", between lines that reach out to either side and turn up at their ends to bracket the row above.
- **The bag and the hand.** Until the turn's draw the acting player's bag, a stack of ship backs in their color with its count, sits in the action area beside the prompt that asks them to click it (for everyone else, beside the waiting text, which names the player being waited on with their name pill: "Waiting for" and the pill); the prompt's own line above stays empty but keeps its height. The room kept for the prompt beside the bag is the width of the three cards that will replace it, so the bag does not move when the prompt goes; the prompt itself stays on one line and may run a little past that room. Clicking the bag commits the draw, and the hand animator owns what follows: the prompt beside the bag goes, each drawn ship deals out of the bag's face into the hand's row in turn, lifting and turning face up as it flies (a dealt ship carries no back, so it is unseen until its face comes round), the bag's count drops with a pop as each ship leaves, and once the last has landed the bag fades and closes up so the hand slides into the center. Then the state shows the hand where the ships landed. Each ship in hand is a card with the bag's outline (`ShipCard`): the ship in its owner's color, its name and its level; the chosen one is edged white with a glow, one with nowhere to go is dimmed and struck through. The hand is the whole of the turn's draw and stands while a ship is still held: a ship that leaves it is not taken away but turns face down where it lies, lifting as it turns; undoing that turns it face up again. When the last ship leaves, the hand fades out together (350ms) after that ship's turn, before the state shows what the turn asks for next. When a special piece is played instead, the whole hand goes back in the bag and fades out face up, unturned, since a turned card means a ship thrown out or placed. A card's back is its owner's constellation over their color's dark ground, one star lit in their color: Scorpius for red, Orion for orange, the Plow for yellow, Cassiopeia for pink and the Serpent for white (`CardBack`). Face-down cards cannot be clicked. Only the drawing player sees the ships fly; other players see the count fall and the bag go. In history the hand of whoever's turn is being looked at stands beside the description of what happened, read-only (`Hand`, shared with live play): stepping through states turns cards and brings the bag and hand back in under 200ms, and a full-action replay deals and turns them as live play does. Whatever else the turn offers while a hand is on show (a note about a global in force, a technology or event choice, a power, a button such as Hold position or End turn) stands in a column to the right of the cards, never on a row above or below them, so the cards are never pushed out of the bar's fixed height; the column opens out from no width over 350ms, sliding the cards left as it grows, and fades in behind it (a CSS transition on the column's own width, as for the special pieces offered after the hand; the cards' wrappers are the hand animator's alone to move, and reduced motion opens it at once). With no hand on show the same content takes the rows under the prompt.
- **Choosing a planet's technology.** The planet being surveyed carries the turning ring while the choice is open. Each card turned up is a name button (gold with a star when elevated); hovering or focusing it shows a popup with its ability and, top right, a small planet of each color it matches (`PlanetBadge`, the board's own planet tile at badge size), and a click chooses it. On touch the first tap shows the popup and a second tap chooses. Cards turned up and set aside for lacking the planet's color are not shown here; the history entry for the survey lists them (see History rationale).
- **Terraforming.** A placed terraformer (a ring around two peaks) beside planets no other terraformer touches holds the turn: the board marks those planets with the turning ring, and with more than one a click picks the planet (the same origin stage the two-ended powers use, so Undo puts it down); with one it is picked without a stage. The action panel then offers the four other colors, each labeled with its category and drawn in its color, and Leave it.
- **Special pieces.** The ten granted pieces beyond hubs, relays and terraformers are drawn as outlines in the player's color (`SpecialShape`): a barred ring inside a thinner guard ring for the Interdictor, the guard ring standing still with six spikes pointing at the six hexes it closes and the barred ring turning counterclockwise, against every other piece's turn, once in twenty-four seconds (still under reduced motion), a pinwheel for the Nebula Seeder (a small core with two spiral arms of cloud that swell and fade as they wind outward, turning slowly, as wide as a Station Hub's ring), a ring for a Gate, a dashed globe for an Artificial Planet in hand, a struck-through probe inside a cordon for the Quarantine Beacon (kin to the Interdictor: a dashed ring turning slowly, a short barrier standing toward each of the six neighboring hexes, the six brightening in turn round the ring, and at the center a small probe with a bar across it), a shield for the Bastion, a scrambler for the Jammer (a core inside two broken rings that turn slowly against each other; on the board, every nine seconds a broken ring of its owner's color runs out from it and fades at the edge of its two-hex reach, a second close behind), a long hull with three bays for the Carrier, a lens for the Deep Scanner and a factory ship for the Salvage Drone (nose up: a spine with a pair of holds either side of it twice over, and an engine block with two nozzles astern; three lights run down the spine from bow to stern and the engines flicker; on the board, every eleven seconds a swarm of specks in its owner's color wanders out from it on jagged, separate tracks, turns at the hexes two away and comes back along the same tracks, fading as it returns). Nebula hexes are filled violet with soft cloud discs. Every open hex beside an interdictor, anyone's, carries a standing faint red tint for everyone at all times, so the ground it holds reads from the board, with lines drawn above every tile: the outside of the whole region (the tinted hexes and the interdictor's own) in a stronger red, the lines between its hexes fainter; its look never changes: while a ship is in hand, the hexes an enemy interdictor closes to it keep the tint and lines they always have and do not take the red dashed outline of the other closed hexes, and the prompt says the red hexes are closed to your ships. When a seeder may lay, its candidate hexes carry the turning ring and the panel offers to leave the nebula be; an artificial planet awaiting its color carries the ring and the panel offers the five colors.
- **Relays and sight links.** A relay is a diamond between signal arcs and takes part in everything drawn for station sight. Every sight link, between two stations or through a relay, is a faint dotted hairline carrying a signal: a ring bursts from one station, a short dash runs each link in turn, and every station or relay that passes it on bursts again, one link every 0.6s in a 4s cycle (`sightSignal` in `utils/sightSignal.ts`). Every pulse and burst runs on the page's clock (`onSharedClock`), so a link added later joins the same cycle. The dashed relay lines on hovering a probe or the relay, and the red dashed outlines on hexes closed to a rival probe, include relays too. Closed hexes are outlined in a layer above every tile, so the whole outline shows; the prompt calls them the red hexes. A relay's lines run past ships.
- **History rows.** As play adds an entry its row fades in over 200ms and the rows below settle over 100ms (local presentation). While History View is open nothing fades or slides: stepping through history shows the list as it stood.
- **History entries.** An entry reads as one flowing sentence: the words follow the player's name pill on its line and wrap where they must, with any player pills and technology chips inline among them. A long description never drops whole to the line under the name.
- **History claims.** A history entry that broke or restored a pending claim names the claimant as a player pill (possessive, so "your" or "their" where that is who it was) and the technology as a technology chip: hovering or clicking the chip opens its popup and draws the dashed trace to the claim's planet, as the player panel's pending chips do.
- **History rationale.** A history entry whose action has a rule to explain carries a small "Rules" button (reading "Hide rules" while open) that opens a bulleted list of why it came out as it did (station complex size, chaining and relays, a hub doubling its own placement, trade routes set up, ended and taken over, shot outcomes, claims paying, lapsing, breaking or coming back, surveying, terraforming, the end-of-turn pause), built from the action's input and metadata by `rationaleFor` in `utils/rationale.ts`. Each entry opens and closes on its own. A survey that turned up cards the planet could not take carries a Set aside button beside it, which opens those cards (turned over on the way and put back under the deck, not discarded) as dimmed chips with the usual popup (`setAsideBy` in `utils/setAside.ts`, read from the survey's `flipped` and `candidates` metadata); the two buttons open independently.
- **Ending a turn.** Once the ships are placed, a turn whose player still holds a technology with something to act on waits: the prompt says so, the usable technologies stay in the action panel as ever, and an End turn button ends it. Using one resolves as it would mid-turn and then asks again.
- **Colony Ship.** A chip among the turn's powers while the player controls an unsettled planet. Choosing it rings those planets; clicking one settles it. A settled planet carries a small station, in its owner's color, at its center for the rest of the game, whoever comes to control it. The points it scores rise from the planet as a placed station's do, and History gives the complex it joined.
- **Technology piles.** A button under the side panel reads how many cards are in the draw pile and the discard. Hovering it, or clicking to pin it, opens a list of the discard above it: each technology by name, with a small planet tile for every planet type the card belongs to (the same tile as on the board, not the color's name) and a count when more than one copy lies there.
- **Factory Override.** A chip among the turn's powers once the draft's discard is made, reading "draws two more ships to place". One click uses it, with nothing to choose and no Undo afterwards: the two ships join the hand as cards after the turn's own, the bag's count falls by two, and History names what was drawn.
- **Conscription.** A chip among the turn's powers once a ship has been discarded this turn, reading when it may be played and "your discard goes back in your bag". One click uses it, with nothing to choose; the bag's count rises and History names the ship taken back. The chip is absent on a turn with no discard and while its three-turn timer runs.
- **Asteroid Field and Security Breach.** Both are chips among the turn's powers. Choosing Asteroid Field shows the three ships it could build as ship cards beside the hand (level 3 probe, level 3 fighter, station), and clicking one builds three of it; History tells the player what they built and everyone else only that three ships went into the bag. Choosing Security Breach shows each opponent as a player pill after "Scrap for", and clicking one sends the Scrap; History names that opponent. Choosing either chip again, or Undo, backs out before the choice is made.
- **Scrap.** A Scrap in hand is a ship card like any other, in its owner's color, drawn as a snapped hull plate and named Scrap. In the draft it is clicked to throw it out, like any ship. After the draft a single click plays it, with no hex to choose: the card turns face down in the hand as a placed ship's does, and nothing happens on the board. Its tooltip then reads "Play the Scrap: it does nothing, but it is out of your bag". It is never drawn on the board.
- **Orbit dots.** Probes in a planet's orbit are shown as small dots circling it, one in each color with any probe there however many that player has; the dots are spaced evenly round the planet. A probe going into orbit flies from its hex, shrinking, to its player's dot.
- **Crowded marks.** When a choice other than an ordinary placement offers more than six hexes, the turning rings give way to still marks: an open hex carries a dot in the acting player's color with a faint halo, and a hex holding a planet or a piece a plain yellow ring without teeth that does not turn. With six hexes or fewer the turning ring is used as before. A chosen near end keeps its gray turning ring, and the Railgun's targets and a planet being explored keep the yellow one.
- **A power being played.** While a power is being played its chip in the turn's powers carries a white edge and a glow in the player's color that swells and settles every 0.9s (a steady glow under reduced motion), and its second line says what to do next. The column beside the hand is padded so neither the chip's edge nor the glow is cut off.
- **The hand's side column.** When choices, powers or special pieces open beside the cards, the cards slide straight across to make room: the column is laid out at its full width from the start and revealed by its opening track, so the row's height does not change on the way and the cards neither dip nor rise.
- **Notes above the hand.** The turn's notes (probes gone into orbit, a global in force) always stand on rows of their own under the prompt and above the bag or hand, never in the column beside the cards, so a note arriving does not move the cards sideways. No room is kept for them while there are none: each note slides open over 200ms (local presentation) and the bag or hand slides down with it. When the bag appears on the player's own turn, the page and the action area scroll just far enough to show the whole bag, if it is not already in view.
- **Probes going into orbit.** When a probe leaves the board for a planet's orbit, and until the acting player next plays a piece (places a ship or special piece, lays a tile, or plays the Scrap), a line under the prompt says so to every player: whose probe or probes went into orbit, and the technology gained by the claim that paid (or discovered, for an elevated technology taking effect at once) as a technology chip that opens the usual popup on hover, focus or click. The history entry says the same.
- **One-shot or timer.** Every technology popup, a chip's and a planet's on the board, says under the ability how often the technology may be used when it is one the player uses: "One-shot" (used once, on a turn of their choosing, then spent) or "Timer" (kept, and resting three of their turns after each use). A technology that grants a piece, and an elevated event or rule, carries neither.
- **Popups.** Every hover popup (a technology chip's, a Rules pill's, a player panel's, the technology piles', and the board's own for planets and special pieces) has a background 75% opaque, so the table shows faintly through it. Those outside the board (the chip's, the pill's, the panel's and the piles') also blur what lies behind them slightly, to keep their text easy to read; the board's own are drawn in the board and do not.
- **The draft's prompt.** While a drawn ship must be discarded the prompt reads "First, discard one of your drawn cards from the game.", so that the discard is plainly the turn's first act. It changes with the situation: "Now discard one more…" for a Deep Scanner's second discard, "…back into your bag." under Global Recycling, and "…, or use Logistics Upgrade to keep them all." while it is ready. Under Overdrive there is no discard and the prompt goes straight to placing, with Overdrive's note under it.
- **Rules pills.** While it is the player's turn and a decision is open that has a note written for it, a small round blue "?" pill follows the prompt on the prompt's own line. Hovering or focusing it opens a popup under it, in the style of a technology chip's, with a title, one sentence on what is being decided and two to four short points that state the rules bearing on it and give no advice on what to choose; a click or tap pins the popup open until a click elsewhere. The pill follows the decision, and every decision has one: stowing a ship and the first station at the opening, the draw (where the bag's prompt stays on one line with the pill at its right, running a little past the space the three cards will take rather than wrapping, so the bag does not move when the cards are dealt), the draft's discard, choosing which ship to place, placing a probe, a fighter, a station or the ship that follows a laid tile, placing each special piece (its own rules, and what playing it costs), surveying or skipping, choosing a planet's technology, firing or holding fire, a torpedo's strike, terraforming, seeding nebula, founding a planet, laying a tile, an event's order and each event's pick, the free drift, each power while it is being played (its technology's text and the rule its use turns on), and ending a turn with a technology still usable. When several apply, what the game is waiting on is explained before what the player merely could do. Two notes stand apart from any decision: the captions under the row of elevated technologies and under the row of special units in play each carry a "?" pill of the same kind, shown on anyone's turn, explaining what those cards are and how they come into play. A "Rules help: on/off" button in the game's header turns every pill off or on; the choice is remembered in the browser and starts on.
- **Board reading aids.** Hovering a special piece on the board (a tap on touch) opens a popup beside it, in the style of a planet's technology popup, naming the piece and its owner and giving the rules its card gave. Hovering a hex holding a piece shows control dots from that piece's owner into every planet beside it that the owner controls. Hovering a probe also shows a dashed relay line to each of its own stations it has clear sight of, and hovering a station shows one to each probe it relays; a relayed probe cannot be attacked. Hovering a planet shows its technology in a popup, headed by the technology's category in that category's color; a planet with no technology yet (unexplored, as when choosing one to survey) shows its kind instead: the category its color belongs to and what that category's techs do. Clicking a planet pins the popup. On touch a tap on a hex the session does not act on inspects it the same way until the board changes.
- **Trade routes.** A planet in a route carries, always, one crate per player with a route to it, in that player's color, in a row centered along the bottom of its hex in turn order. Hovering a crate draws that player's routes to the planet (a slowly drifting dashed line in the joiner's color, rings around both planets) and names who holds it; hovering or tapping a joined planet draws all of its routes beside its technology popup. A crate is there only while its route stands: it goes the moment the owner's chain is broken, and comes back in the color of whoever sets the route up next. A route a rival's standing chain took over floats its points over that rival's planet, in the rival's color.
- **Pending claims.** While a claim would still pay (the claimant still leads), each of the claimant's probes beside the planet shines a soft cone of light onto it that fades out over the planet and slowly breathes, and the claimant's player panel lists the technology as a dashed Pending chip. Hovering or pinning that chip draws a slowly drifting dashed line from its popup to a ring around the planet.
- **The bag.** The bag of whoever's turn it is shows in the action area until the turn's draw: three stacked ship backs, the front one in the player's color with that color's own fighter silhouette, the ships left and "in bag". On the owner's own turn it is a button with a white edge and a glow in their color, and the prompt beside it says to click it to draw; otherwise it only reports, a little dimmed. Its going and the deal that follows are the hand animator's (see The bag and the hand). It is not shown in History View.
- **Fighters by color.** Each player color flies its own fighter silhouette, on the board, the placement ghost, the torpedo and the bag (`utils/fighterShapes.ts`): red a kite with an open V trail, orange a notched delta with two pale windows along its nose, yellow a cruciform, pink a swallowtail, white a needle. Levels keep the same formations of one, two or three ships.
- **Color schemes.** A "Colors: classic / shared / bold" button in the game's header, beside Rules help, moves on to the next of three schemes at each click, for this player alone, remembered in the browser and starting on classic. Classic is warm player colors with the planets in five cool colors of their own (periwinkle, aqua, mint, teal, violet). Shared gives each kind of planet one of the five player colors, so the table shows five colors in all: red for the crescent planet (Belligerence), orange for the twin planet (Mobility), yellow for the ringed planet (Acquisitions), pink for the pulsing planet (Expansion) and white for the planet with a moon (Espionage). Bold redraws the players in deep, saturated colors (the red, orange, yellow, pink and white seats become red, orange, green, purple and blue, with white words on their pills, ribbons and cards) and the planets in soft light colors whose hues sit a little askew from those: soft magenta for the crescent planet, sky for the twin planet, soft teal for the ringed planet, peach for the pulsing planet and butter for the planet with a moon. A player using the colorblind palette keeps that palette's player colors in every scheme. Everything drawn in a planet's or a player's color follows the choice at once: board tiles and pieces, planet icons, category headings, elevated and unit cards, route-power dots, controlled-planet dots, name pills, bags and card backs. A planet's kind is its shape, the same in every scheme, so no text anywhere names a planet by color: history lines, the rules behind them, the survey warning, the route-power popups and the founding and terraforming choices show the planet's icon instead. The founding and terraforming choices are dark buttons edged in the planet's color, each holding the planet's icon and its category's name.
- **Sky.** A starfield, the same for every player at the table and different from game to game, lies behind the whole table (board, action area and side panels) and stops at the table's edges: it is drawn inside the game's own area, under the table, and never behind the rest of the page. Its bright band, tilted across the sky, is three loose strands of stars winding around one another, none of them regular: they cross unevenly and leave dark gaps of differing size and shape between them, each strand thickening, thinning and nearly breaking along its length, over a thin haze of stars. The band's glow is soft throughout, pools of light that fade to nothing at their edges, wide across the whole band and strung along each strand; no line of light is drawn. A few dark lanes cross the band, and some stars twinkle. The still sky is drawn once as a single image, and only the stars that twinkle are elements of their own over it: a sky of live shapes was redrawn under every animation crossing it, which slowed Firefox to under a frame a second.
- **Turn header.** The header above the board names only History View and End of game; whose turn it is shows on the player cards (the arrow, glow and ribbon of the player to move) and in the site's own turn stripe, so the header carries no turn text.
- **Player panel.** Each player card is dark with a ribbon across the top in the player's color holding the name (an arrow before it, and a glow on the card and badge, for the player to move) and the ships left in the bag; the score sits in a round badge breaking out of the ribbon's right corner; a line below shows the planets held as dots in the planets' colors with their count; the technology chips are tinted rectangles edged in the player's color, a pending claim dashed, with pieces to place as a solid marker in that color.
- **Player panel score.** Each player card shows one figure, the points the game would award now (banked plus planets held) and the ships left in the bag. Hovering, focusing or clicking the figure opens a breakdown for every player under the card: rank, points from station complexes and from chains (read back from the history, with an Other column only when the history does not account for the banked total), the planets held, and the total; clicking pins it and clicking elsewhere unpins.
- **Survey warning.** While the player chooses whether to survey, a muted line under the prompt (styled as the lines about globals in force) appears when the probe is the only thing stopping a rival's pending claim on a planet beside it that the player cannot survey: it names the rival as a player pill and the planet's color, and says that surveying commits the probe elsewhere and that skipping keeps the block. One line per such claim.
- **Claim beams through gates.** A probe staking a claim on a planet it reaches through a gate pair has its beam in two legs: from the planet into the gate beside it, and out of the twin gate into the probe, never one line across the board. When the claim pays, the probe's own leg draws back into the probe; the planet's leg goes with the state.
- **Why the game ended.** Once the game is over, the history's top entry reads "The game has ended." followed by the reason: the player who played the last piece from their bag, or the player who drew a ship with nowhere legal to place it.
- **Asteroid Field's choice.** The three ship cards stay on show after one is clicked: the two passed over fade, then the chosen card twirls once, lifting a little, and fades, exactly as the stowed ship does in the opening (one shared motion, on the action's timeline, so the state moves on only when it has played). The cards are held on show by the session (`asteroidCardsHeld`) from the click until the state moves on, since no power counts as in progress while the action is being applied; they cannot be clicked again meanwhile. Undo, history and reload show no motion.
- **Planets held.** The "N planets held" line in a player panel is a button. Hovering it rings every planet that player controls with the turning toothed ring, in that player's color (never yellow, which is kept for hexes the acting player may choose); a click keeps the rings up until the button is clicked again or anything else is clicked. The rings are drawn in the marks layer, above every piece and below the popups. Which player is shown is held in the session (`heldPlanetsShownFor`), so the panel and the board agree. One player's planets at a time: a hover on another panel shows that player's while it lasts.
- **Every color.** While a player controls a planet of each of the five colors, their panel carries an "Every color" chip beside their route powers, with "+2 each turn" as its second line and the five planets in its popup. It is there for exactly as long as the condition holds. The points themselves arrive with the player's draw, which the history says, and are counted in the score popup's Bonus column with bounties.
- **Player panel route powers.** Each trade route power a player holds now is a chip in their panel, above their technologies, in the player's color like a technology chip but led by a dot in the power's planet color. Its popup is the technology popup with the category's name and "power" as its heading, the power's name and ability, and a last line saying how it is held (a route of that color, control of enough planets of it, or both). The chip is there for exactly as long as the power is.
- **Player panel technologies.** Each held technology is a chip that explains itself in a popup on hover, focus or click (click pins it; clicking elsewhere unpins). A cooldown still waiting shows three pips, one lit per round still to wait; they stay full until the owner's next turn begins. Once it can be used again the pips give way to a dot and the word "READY", both in bright yellow. In the player's own panel, a technology that could be used this moment (any the action area offers as a chip: a ready cooldown or an unused once-only technology) carries a thin white ring and plays on a click exactly as its action-area chip does; its popup adds "Click to use it." (on touch the first tap opens the popup and says "Tap again to use it.", and the second plays it). Rivals' chips, and the player's own outside those moments, only explain themselves. A once-only technology leaves the panel once it is used (the player still holds it for the rules, so its planet is not offered to them again). A player with none shows an inert No technologies currently chip. A technology that grants a piece (Station Upgrade, Terraforming, Relay Network) carries an "N to place" marker on its chip while its pieces wait in hand, and its popup says so; the pieces have no chips of their own.
- **Turn header and action area.** Together they take a fixed 180px at every width, so nothing shown there ever moves or rescales the board; content taller than that scrolls inside the action area, and is a design problem to fix rather than a reason to grow it. The header reads History or End of game on the left and carries no turn text (see Turn header); at its right are Overview (while rules help is on), the rules help switch and the single Undo. In History View the action area shows the History Step that produced the displayed state instead of the prompt and controls. Once the game has a result (outside History View) the action area shows the game-end summary instead: the winners, called a shared win when tied, and every player ranked by final score with the end-of-game planet points beside the total; tied players share a rank.

## Coexistence and precedence

- Highlight precedence: an elevated event's pick wins over everything, then a seeder's hexes, then an artificial planet awaiting its color; otherwise a chosen power (Shift, then Spoofed Orders, then Feint, then Wormhole, then Download, then Updated Logistics) wins over a tile to lay, which wins over the ship owed on a new tile, a technology move, survey candidates, attack targets and finally ordinary placement. A higher layer that is active but has no hexes still owns the board (a tile with no room at this rotation highlights nothing rather than falling through). Placement hexes, owed or ordinary, are the engine's legal placements for the ship in hand, so a probe never highlights a hex two enemy stations watch. Board clicks follow the same order in `onBoardClick`. Only one power can be chosen at a time; choosing another replaces it.
- Choosing a ship to place drops a chosen power, because the ship stage comes first in the staged selection; choosing a power keeps the chosen ship.
- Spread and Railgun exist only while a shot is pending, which excludes placement, tile laying and the other powers; neither is offered for a pending torpedo strike. Torpedo exists only while a fighter is to be placed, and is the one power that keeps the placement preview.
- Hover wins over the touch preview for the placement ghost; the inspected hex never drives the ghost, only the reading aids.
- During any state transition (`busy`) and in History View no staged choice applies, so only state-derived marks are drawn.

## Shared visual state

All staged choices live in one staged selection (`model/stagedSelection.ts`, stage order piece, power, rotation, origin, targets, preview) on the shared helpers. Each is manual except an origin chosen automatically when a power has a single near end. Undo pops the highest manual stage; only when none is left does it undo the last action. There is no Back or cancel control.

## Render ownership

- **Pieces.** Pieces render in one layer above every hex with no hit target, so the hex underneath takes clicks and a piece can slide across the board. Each piece element is keyed by piece and hex, so a moved piece mounts afresh at its destination on the state swap.
- **Terraforming.** `TerraformAnimator` draws each recolored planet in its new color with its motion held at its starting pose (so the real tile, mounting its new color's motion from the start, takes over without a jump), clipped to a rect that widens from the hex's left edge, while the board's own tile is clipped to the rest of the hex, shrinking in step, so the old color is wiped away rather than showing through; a white scan line clipped to the hex runs along the edge, on `actionTimeline` (0.9s with an action, 0.15s plus a 0.05s fade without), then clears the copies after the animations.
- **Board frame.** The board's viewBox follows its extent (the hexes, plus a tile's ghost footprints while one is being laid), and the frame animator alone writes it (`FrameAnimator`, `frameBoard`). When a change of state changes the extent (a tile laid, or undone) the frame slides to the new state's extent with that change, on the shared action timeline at position 0: 450ms with an action, 150ms without, so the new state arrives already framed. When only a tile's ghosts come and go (hovering a place for it, rotating it) nothing has been committed, and the frame slides on its own over 450ms, a local tween killed when a change of state takes the frame over or the board is torn down; while a change of state owns the frame, the latest extent asked for is kept and followed once it is done. Reduced motion sets the local frame at once.
- **The opening.** Before the first turn each player, last in turn order first, stows one extra ship in their bag (`StowingShip`) and then places one free station (`PlacingOpeningStation`). While stowing, the panel shows the acting player the three ships they may stow as cards in their color (a level 3 probe, a level 3 fighter, a station). When live play brings the cards (the board assembly ending for the first to open, or the action that ends the opening of the player before) they fade in one at a time face down, left to right (350ms each, 220ms apart), then turn face up from left to right (500ms each, 160ms apart, lifting as they turn), and cannot be clicked until the last has turned (`StowChoices`, a local timeline killed on teardown). Cards that come back any other way (the choice undone, History View left, the page reloaded) are face up and ready at once, as they are under reduced motion. Clicking one commits the choice and locks the cards. For the stowing player alone the action's own motion follows on the shared timeline, before the state moves on: the other two cards fade out (300ms), the chosen card twirls once right round, back to face (900ms, lifting), then fades out (350ms); with no action (undo, state-only history) nothing plays, and the cards return by mounting, face up. Nobody else is sent the choice or shown the cards. There is no way to decline, and nothing on the board is clickable. Everyone else reads that the player is stowing a ship and never learns which: the history line names the ship for its own player alone ("stowed an extra level 3 fighter in their bag, unseen by the others") and reads "stowed an extra ship in their bag, unseen" for the rest. For the station, the panel's prompt names the player placing (or asks the viewer to place on any open hex, saying it scores nothing now), the hand and bag stay hidden, no special piece is offered, and the legal hexes are every open hex. The station carries a faint dotted orbit in its owner's color, turning once in ninety seconds (still under reduced motion), with none of the hub's pods, so the hub's ring stays the hub's; the orbit also shows on the ghost preview and in history. As it lands, two dotted orbits spread from the hex and fade (`ArrivalAnimator`, actionTimeline, 900ms with the second 200ms behind), only when an action is present; undo and state-only history show it in place. History reads "placed their free opening station". The opening placements do not count as acting for the board assembly intro, so a player who has placed but not yet drawn still sees it.
- **Arrivals.** `ArrivalAnimator` draws every piece an action placed (by a new piece id) at its hex from the start of the change until the new state is shown, so a placed ship never vanishes while the score float, girder growth, blasts or any other animation of that change runs. The same holds across a run of actions shown as one change (a placement and the automatic pass after it, say): the stand-ins for placed ships, grown girders and recolored planets are worked out against the board on show, not each step's own start, so what an earlier step put down stays drawn, already complete, while a later step animates. It replaces the girder animator's own arriving-station stand-in.
- **Piece moves.** `PieceMoveAnimator` slides every piece that changes hexes (Tactical Shift, Spoofed Orders, a drift step, Wormhole, Tactical Feint and their undo) on `actionTimeline` (450ms with an action, 150ms without).
- **Girders.** `TrussGrowthAnimator` fades girders the transition breaks and grows the ones it makes from the end that changed, drawing a transient station only for a station new to the board. With an action the growth runs on `finalTimeline`, after pieces finish moving (500ms); without one everything runs at once (150ms).
- **Torpedo strike.** `TorpedoAnimator` (actionTimeline) turns the torpedo to face its station over 300ms and runs it into the station over the next 400ms, where it vanishes. It moves the fighter's own node when the board draws it, and otherwise draws a torpedo of its own for the step: a placement struck at once replays as two steps while the board still shows the state before both, so the second step has no node to move. `BlastAnimator` holds the strike's single, larger blast at the station until that impact, so the strike runs to about 1.5s. With no action the torpedo simply comes or goes with the state.
- **Blasts and score floats.** `BlastAnimator` (actionTimeline, up to 780ms) and `ScoreFloatAnimator` (finalTimeline, 1.6s: one unlabeled pill rising from the arrival hex with everything the action scored summed, since history has the details) draw action-scoped transients only when an action is present: live play, other players' actions and full-action replay. Undo and state-only history show the result directly. Their transients clear in `afterAnimations`.
- **Board assembly.** When a player first opens a game they have not yet acted in (a spectator: a game nobody has acted in), the board's tiles drop into place one by one in the order setup grew them (the first tile, then each tile where it joined the cluster), each shrinking down onto the table over 550ms with a 220ms stagger, and as the last lands everything on the table (pieces, girders, routes, every mark) fades in together over 600ms. While it plays, and until Play is chosen on the title, the action panel says and offers nothing: the prompt's line and the stow cards' row keep their height but stay empty (`boardAssembling` on the session, set by the board and cleared by Play), and the prompt appears the moment Play is chosen.
- **Title.** On that same first opening the game's name stands over the board while it builds: a small four-pointed star opens out above it and goes on twinkling, the letters of EARENDEL come out of a blur one after another (1.1s each, 140ms apart), and a thin line draws out from the middle beneath them, all over a soft dark patch so the name reads against the board. When the last of the assembly finishes, two buttons fade in under the name, in room kept for them so the name does not move: Play, which fades the title away over 400ms and begins the game, and Overview, which opens the rules overview over it. Under reduced motion there is no assembly: the title stands still over the finished board with its buttons at once. Local presentation (`titleStage` on the session); nothing in the game changes until Play.
- **Rules overview.** An Overview button in the header (there only while rules help is on), and the one on the title (always there), open the game in eleven cards over the whole table (why the game is called Earendel, the game in brief, probes, fighters, stations, control of planets, trade routes, trade route powers, enhanced technologies, special units, the end of the game), each a title, a sentence and three to five rules-only points (the first is a paragraph alone), in three columns on a wide window, two on a narrower one and one on a phone, scrolling when they do not fit. The table behind is dimmed; each card is see-through, with the table blurred behind it, and tinted by what it is about, with a small label saying so: amber for the game (its name, in brief, the end), blue for ships (probes, fighters, stations), rose for scoring (control, trade routes) and silver for technology (enhanced technologies, special units). None of the tints is a planet's color. Close, a click outside the cards, or Escape closes it; opened from the title, closing returns to the title. Local presentation owned by the board: it explains no transition, only the panel's opening content waits on it, it does not replay from history, reduced motion skips it, and the browser remembers per game and player that it has played. The opening (stowing a ship, placing the free station) does not count as having acted.
- **Continuous cues** (claim beams, sight-link signals, hovered trade-route lines, control dots, relay lines on hover, planet motion, the turning mark ring, target and survey chevrons, the pending-claim link line, hub ring) are local CSS on state-derived marks and explain no transition.
